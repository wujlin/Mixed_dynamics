Reviewer #1

 

This manuscript develops a phase-transition model of collective emotion

centered on the competing feedback between mainstream media and user-generated

content, and examines it through analytical derivation, network simulation, and

Weibo-based empirical analysis. The theoretical part of the paper is relatively

well developed, and the decomposition into polarization direction 𝑞 and activity intensity 𝑎 also offers a

certain degree of novelty. Overall, the topic fits well within the scope of

this journal.

 

I recommend acceptance after minor revisions addressing the following

points:

 

	1. Could

the authors further clarify whether the empirical section is intended as a test

of theoretically predicted signatures, rather than a direct identification of

the mixed-feedback mechanism itself? The current empirical results appear to

focus mainly on the relationships between activity and polarization jumps, and

between r_proxy and volatility.

	2. Could

the correspondence between the theoretical parameter 𝑟 and the empirical proxy r_proxy be

discussed in greater detail? If 𝑟 is

meant to capture the effective balance between feedback channels, it may be

helpful to explain more explicitly to what extent a post-count-based proxy can

represent the actual information environment experienced by users.

	3.

Could the discussion give somewhat more emphasis to the specific contribution

of the direction–intensity decomposition? In particular, the interpretation of

activity 𝑎 as

moderate-buffer depletion, and the link to critical slowing down as a potential

early-warning signal, seem central to the manuscript’s theoretical value and

may deserve even clearer positioning relative to existing polarization studies.
